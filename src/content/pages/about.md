# About Harold Ricker

### Certified Senior Transition Specialist, Newtree Senior Transition Services

---

## Caring for People, Land, and Home

<picture class="bio-photo"><source srcset="/images/harold-ricker.webp" type="image/webp" /><img src="/images/harold-ricker.jpg" width="405" height="400" alt="Harold Ricker" /></picture>

My first experience with real estate was a land joint venture with friends here in Northern California. From there I spent 25 years in forestry, much of it in Oregon: logging, tree planting, and eventually work as a forestry technician. That work taught me a great deal about land and how large properties are managed over time.

I later went back to school, starting at College of the Redwoods and earning a B.S. in Computer Information Systems from Cal Poly Humboldt. I then spent 11 years as a programmer analyst for the County of Humboldt. Most of that time I worked in the social services branch, supporting Adult Protective Services and the county's senior programs. I learned how county government works from the inside and built working relationships across the local agencies that serve older adults.

Caregiving has been part of my life the whole way. While I was in school, I cared for a wonderful married couple who were both living with serious disabilities, and I stayed with them until they passed. While I was with the county, a friend became housebound, and I was her caregiver for several years. She now receives specialized care, and we are still close. Today I am an In-Home Supportive Services (IHSS) provider through Humboldt County, and a member of SEIU, caring for another good friend.

In April 2025 I founded Newtree Capital Resources as a private money lender, which brought me back into real estate and investing. Seeing where caregiving, county services, and property meet led me to Mom's House, and in 2026 I became a Certified Senior Transition Specialist.

Newtree Senior Transition Services brings all of that together. I know what it's like to care for someone at home, how local programs actually work, and how to turn a long-held family home into the resources a senior needs, without adding to the family's stress.
